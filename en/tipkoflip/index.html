TipkoFlip

Type a message and show it to the other person in large letters, face to face. When you can't hear or hear poorly, when you can't speak or your voice is weak – and for anyone who sometimes finds writing easier, for example in a noisy place.

  • No ads
  • No account
  • Works offline
  • For Android

Coming soon to Google Play · the app is now in closed testing.

Where TipkoFlip helps

Small everyday moments where a short message in large letters does what explaining can't.

At the counter

Behind glass and in the noise, hearing and lip-reading are hard. Type what you need, "Hello, I'm collecting a parcel", and the clerk reads it straight away in large letters.

At the bakery

Type exactly what you want, "A chocolate croissant, please", and get the right one, not something else.

A neighbour who doesn't realise you can't hear

A message in large letters, readable even without glasses, says more than a long explanation. At last they know to speak slowly, facing you.

Names and titles

Names are the hardest to lip-read. Hand the phone to the other person to type them, and the Swap roles button turns the screen towards them.

A dimly lit restaurant

When the light is low and lip-reading is hard, the other person types what you missed.

When you can't speak

You hear the other person but can't answer out loud. Type your answer and TipkoFlip shows it in large letters or reads it aloud.

When your voice is weak or tired

One-tap phrases for what you often say, so you don't have to type everything again or strain your voice.

Tips

  • Try the sign. Turn the phone sideways and the text fills the whole screen. People take to it well: they are a little surprised at first, and then it's easier for them.
  • For older people and in poor light, make the letters bigger with two fingers or turn on ALL CAPS in Settings.
  • Save sentences you use often in Phrases, and next time show them with one tap.
  • When you don't understand something, hand the phone to the other person to type it.
  • Start with Introduction. Long-press the TipkoFlip icon and choose Introduction, so the other person knows straight away who they are talking to.

What TipkoFlip does

Writing and display

  • Large text across the whole screen, flipped 180° to face the person across from you
  • Two-sided display: each of you types and reads on your own half of the screen
  • Read the typed text aloud
  • A sign across the whole landscape screen (e.g. HELP), with an attention signal
  • Moving sign: the text travels across the screen in one line, three speeds
  • Text size automatic, with a slider or by pinching
  • The screen stays on, and your last message is still there when you reopen the app
  • Swap roles: the screen turns upside down, so you can simply hand the phone over

Phrases

  • Saved sentences for everyday situations, on screen with one tap
  • Your own phrases – add, edit and delete
  • Optional categories and subcategories, e.g. Everyday, Emergency, Counter, Food
  • Long-press the displayed text to save it as a new phrase
  • In parts: a longer message shown part by part, in large letters

Quick access and sharing

  • Your first message to start the conversation: long-press the icon → Introduction
  • A Quick Settings button opens TipkoFlip with a new message
  • Text from other apps (Share or selected text) shown in large letters or saved as a phrase
  • Share phrases and whole categories with others, and move all your phrases to a new phone easily
  • A message as a QR code, so the other person gets it on their own phone

Your way

  • Text and background colours, with a warning if the contrast is too low
  • An easier-to-read font and text in ALL CAPS
  • Optionally bigger buttons and text throughout the app
  • Text aligned left, centre or right
  • Hide the buttons you don't use and put the rest in your own order
  • 35 languages
  • A guide with short animations for each feature, and examples from everyday situations (counter, café, bakery, taxi…)

Split screen

You can open TipkoFlip on a split screen together with a speech-to-text app. You type in TipkoFlip, and what the other person says appears as text in the other app, on the same screen.

Split screen means two apps are open on the phone screen at the same time, one above the other. Most Android phones have it, but many people have never turned it on.

How to turn on split screen

  1. Open TipkoFlip and your speech-to-text app, one after the other.
  2. Open the recent apps view: swipe up from the bottom of the screen and hold, or tap the ▢ (square) button at the bottom.
  3. Tap the icon above the speech-to-text app's window (or press and hold the window) and choose Split screen. It moves to the top.
  4. Then tap TipkoFlip, so it opens in the bottom half.
  5. You can drag the line between the apps up or down. It works best when each takes about half the screen.

The name and place of the option depend on the phone maker, for example “Open in split screen view” or an icon with two rectangles. If you can't find it, search for “split screen” in your phone's settings.

The Split screen button in TipkoFlip opens your chosen app beside TipkoFlip by itself. On some phones, especially older models, this doesn't work – then turn on split screen with the steps above.

TipkoFlip itself uses no microphone and no internet. The speech is recognised by the other app, under its own privacy rules.

Frequently asked questions

General

Is TipkoFlip only for people who can't hear?

No. TipkoFlip also helps when you can hear but can't speak or find speaking hard: you type your answer, and the other person reads it in large letters or TipkoFlip reads it aloud. On first launch you choose what you need most often (I can't hear, I can't speak, Speaking is hard for me, or I can't hear or speak), and your first message and starter phrases follow that choice. You can change it in Settings → My first message.

What is TipkoFlip Plus?

Plus unlocks extra features: two-sided view, moving sign, all colours (the five basic ones are always available), a message as a QR code, longer messages In parts and phrase subcategories. You buy it once, for €2.99, in the app through Google Play, with no subscription. Everything else in TipkoFlip works without Plus.

On a new phone or after reinstalling, open Settings → TipkoFlip Plus → Restore purchase, signed in with the same Google Account.

Does TipkoFlip have ads?

No, TipkoFlip has no ads and needs no account or sign-in.

Where can I download TipkoFlip?

TipkoFlip is currently in closed testing on Google Play. The public release is planned soon.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet, TipkoFlip is Android only. If you would use an iPhone version, let us know. It helps us see how much interest there is.

Typing and display

How do I turn the text towards me?

Tap the round button in the bottom-left corner of the display. The text turns towards you, and another tap turns it back to the other person.

How do I make the letters bigger or smaller?

Spread or pinch two fingers on the big display, or tap the Aa button and move the slider. The Auto button goes back to automatic size, which adjusts to the length of the text.

Can the other person write to me too?

Yes. The Swap roles button turns the screen upside down, so you simply hand them the phone and they write to you.

There's also a two-sided display: Swap roles button → Two-sided display, and each of you types and reads on your own half of the screen.

Why doesn't the screen turn off?

On purpose, so your message stays visible while the other person reads it. When you leave TipkoFlip, the screen turns off as usual.

Is the attention signal safe for the eyes?

The signal slowly alternates white and yellow, twice a second. That is below the limit of three flashes a second recommended by accessibility guidelines. A tap on the screen stops it straight away.

Why doesn't the sign turn sideways on my tablet?

On some tablets and foldables with Android 16 or newer, the system decides the screen orientation and ignores the app. Turn the device by hand and the sign adjusts.

Features and quick access

How do I quickly show a message to start a conversation?

Long-press the TipkoFlip icon and choose Introduction. Your first message is on the big display straight away. You can drag the shortcut onto your home screen and change the text in Settings → My first message.

How do I add TipkoFlip to Quick Settings?

Swipe down from the top of the screen to open Quick Settings, then tap the pencil or Edit. Drag the TipkoFlip tile in among the others. Tapping it opens TipkoFlip with a new message.

How do I hide buttons I don't use?

In Settings → Buttons on the main screen, turn off the buttons you don't need and change the order of the rest with the arrows. Settings can't be hidden, so you can always bring everything back.

Why doesn't read aloud work, or why does it sound odd?

TipkoFlip uses the text-to-speech voice installed on your phone. In your Android settings, search for "text-to-speech" and download a voice for your language.

How do I open TipkoFlip in split screen?

The Split screen button in TipkoFlip's toolbar opens a speech recognition app beside TipkoFlip. The first time, you choose the app; you can change it in Settings → Split screen.

From version 1.7, TipkoFlip has its own lower keyboard in split screen, so the app above keeps its size and its subtitles stay visible while you type.

It doesn't work on every phone, especially older models. Then you can turn on split screen yourself, following the steps in the Conversation both ways section.

Split screen doesn't open. What can I do?

Opening one app beside another doesn't work on every phone, especially older models. Then turn it on yourself: open the speech recognition app, then your recent apps, tap that app's icon and choose split screen, then TipkoFlip. The exact steps are in the Conversation both ways section.

If the phone says the app doesn't support split screen, choose another app, or look in the phone's settings for an option like “Multi window for all apps” (not every phone has it).

Why is the other app at the top (or bottom) and not the other way round?

Which half of the screen the app opened next to TipkoFlip goes to is decided by the phone, not by TipkoFlip. That's why it is at the top on some phones and at the bottom on others.

Both layouts work. With TipkoFlip at the top, its display is closer to the person opposite you and the other person's words are closer to you. If you prefer it the other way round, swap the halves (see the next question).

How do I swap the halves of the screen or change their size?

Drag the line between the apps up or down to resize the halves. Tap the dots on the line to swap them; on some phones you double-tap the line instead.

To leave split screen, drag the line to the edge of the screen.

Can I use my phone's keyboard in split screen?

Yes. In split screen TipkoFlip has its own lower keyboard, so the app beside it keeps its size. Turn on your phone's keyboard (with word suggestions and dictation) in Settings → Split screen, but then the app beside TipkoFlip gets smaller while you type.

Can TipkoFlip turn what the other person says into text?

A speech recognition app can do that in split screen with TipkoFlip. The steps are on this page, in the Split screen section.

Privacy and your data

Do I need internet or an account?

No. TipkoFlip works offline, without signing in, and asks for no permissions you have to approve. Internet is needed only to buy TipkoFlip Plus through Google Play.

What does TipkoFlip save, and does it send anything?

Your phrases, categories and settings are saved only on your phone. Optionally also your last message, which you can turn off in Settings. TipkoFlip sends nothing over the internet and has no analytics. Buying TipkoFlip Plus is handled by Google Play.

More in the privacy policy.

Does TipkoFlip back up my phrases?

If Google backup is turned on on your phone, Android also backs up TipkoFlip's phrases, categories and settings, so they come back by themselves on a new phone when you sign in with the same Google Account. The backup is encrypted with your screen lock (PIN, pattern or password), so Google can't read it. TipkoFlip itself sends nothing over the internet.

If you don't want a backup, turn it off in your phone's settings:

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Look for Backup (the search bar at the top of Settings is easiest). Depending on the phone it's under Google, System or Accounts and backup.
  3. Turn off Backup by Google One (or Back up to Google Drive).

This turns off backup for the whole phone, not just TipkoFlip. You can then move your phrases straight from the old phone when setting up the new one (by cable or Wi-Fi), or with Send all phrases to yourself. More in the Google backup section.

How do I move my phrases to a new phone?

On your old phone

  1. Open Settings → Moving to a new phone and tap Send all phrases to yourself.
  2. A list of apps opens. Choose your e-mail app and send the message to your own address.

On your new phone

  1. Install TipkoFlip.
  2. Open that message. Long-press any word in it, then tap Select all.
  3. In the menu above the text, tap TipkoFlip. If you don't see it, tap ⋮ or Share and choose TipkoFlip from the list of apps.
  4. TipkoFlip opens with your phrases. Tap Add to phrases, then Add.

The phrases go back into their categories. Any you already have are skipped, so nothing is doubled.

Do I lose my phrases when I update the app?

No. Updating keeps all your phrases, categories and settings.

How do I delete all my data?

Uninstall TipkoFlip, or in your Android settings open Apps → TipkoFlip → Storage and tap Clear data. The names differ slightly between phone makers. If Google backup was on, Android removes the copy in your Google Account by itself over time.

Privacy

TipkoFlip doesn't collect your personal data, doesn't send it to us and doesn't share it with anyone. No account, no analytics, no ads. Your phrases and settings stay on your phone, unless Google backup is turned on on your phone.

Google backup

Android can save apps' data to your Google Account, so it comes back by itself when you move to a new phone. This is an Android service, not part of TipkoFlip: TipkoFlip has no internet access and sends nothing itself.

Since version 1.7.9, TipkoFlip follows your phone's setting, like most apps: when Google backup is on on your phone, TipkoFlip's data is backed up too. Backup is turned on or off in your phone's settings (Settings → Backup), for the whole phone.

On: your phrases, categories and settings come back by themselves on a new phone when you sign in with the same Google Account. Google stores and keeps the backup, encrypted with your screen lock, and we have no access to it.

Off: everything stays only on this phone. If the phone breaks or is lost, the phrases can't be recovered. So before you change phones, tap “Send all phrases to yourself” in Settings and choose e-mail or a notes app. A direct transfer from one phone to another, by cable or Wi-Fi when you set up the new phone, works even with backup off, because it doesn't go through Google.
