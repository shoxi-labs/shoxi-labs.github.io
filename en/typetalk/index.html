TypeTalk

Type a message and show it to the other person in large letters, face to face. For deaf and hard of hearing people – and for anyone who sometimes finds it easier to write than to speak or listen, for example in a noisy place.

  • Free
  • No ads
  • No account
  • Works offline
  • For Android

Coming soon to Google Play · the app is now in closed testing.

What TypeTalk does

Writing and display

  • Large text across the whole screen, flipped 180° to face the person across from you
  • Swap roles: the screen turns upside down, so you can simply hand the phone over
  • Read the typed text aloud
  • A sign across the whole landscape screen (e.g. HELP), with an attention signal
  • Text size automatic, with a slider or by pinching
  • The screen stays on, and your last message is still there when you reopen the app

Phrases

  • Saved sentences for everyday situations, on screen with one tap
  • Your own phrases – add, edit and delete
  • Optional categories and subcategories, e.g. Everyday, Emergency, Counter, Food
  • Long-press the displayed text to save it as a new phrase

Quick access and sharing

  • Your first message to start the conversation: long-press the icon → Introduction
  • A Quick Settings button opens TypeTalk with a new message
  • Text from other apps (Share or selected text) shown in large letters or saved as a phrase
  • Share phrases and whole categories with others, and move all your phrases to a new phone easily
  • A message as a QR code, so the other person gets it on their own phone

Your way

  • Text and background colours, with a warning if the contrast is too low
  • An easier-to-read font and text in ALL CAPS
  • Optionally bigger buttons and text throughout the app
  • Text aligned left, centre or right
  • Hide the buttons you don't use and put the rest in your own order
  • Croatian and English, with a built-in user guide

Conversation both ways

You can open TypeTalk on a split screen together with a speech-to-text app. You type in TypeTalk, and what the other person says appears as text in the other app, on the same screen.

Split screen means two apps are open on the phone screen at the same time, one above the other. Most Android phones have it, but many people have never turned it on.

How to turn on split screen

  1. Open TypeTalk and your speech-to-text app, one after the other.
  2. Open the recent apps view: swipe up from the bottom of the screen and hold, or tap the ▢ (square) button at the bottom.
  3. Tap the icon above the speech-to-text app's window (or press and hold the window) and choose Split screen. It moves to the top.
  4. Then tap TypeTalk, so it opens in the bottom half.
  5. You can drag the line between the apps up or down. It works best when each takes about half the screen.

The name and place of the option depend on the phone maker, for example “Open in split screen view” or an icon with two rectangles. If you can't find it, search for “split screen” in your phone's settings.

TypeTalk itself still uses no microphone and no internet. The speech is recognised by the other app, under its own privacy rules.

Frequently asked questions

General

Is TypeTalk free? Does it have ads?

Yes, it's free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. Every feature is available to everyone.

Where can I download TypeTalk?

TypeTalk is currently in closed testing on Google Play. The public release is planned after version 1.9.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet, TypeTalk is Android only. If you would use an iPhone version, let us know. It helps us see how much interest there is.

Which languages is the app in?

Croatian and English. Version 1.7 adds 14 more languages, including Slovenian, Serbian, Bosnian, Montenegrin, Macedonian and German.

Which phones does it work on?

Phones and tablets with Android 8.0 or newer.

How do I suggest a feature or report a bug?

Write to shoxilabs.dev@gmail.com. Many of TypeTalk's features came from user suggestions.

Typing and display

How do I turn the text towards me?

Tap the round button in the bottom-left corner of the display. The text turns towards you, and another tap turns it back to the other person.

How do I make the letters bigger or smaller?

Spread or pinch two fingers on the big display, or tap the Aa button and move the slider. The Auto button goes back to automatic size, which adjusts to the length of the text.

Can the other person write to me too?

Yes. The Swap roles button turns the screen upside down, so you simply hand them the phone and they write to you.

Version 1.8 brings a two-sided display: the phone lies between you, and each of you types on your own half of the screen.

Why doesn't the screen turn off?

On purpose, so your message stays visible while the other person reads it. When you leave TypeTalk, the screen turns off as usual.

Is the attention signal safe for the eyes?

The signal slowly alternates white and yellow, twice a second. That is below the limit of three flashes a second recommended by accessibility guidelines. A tap on the screen stops it straight away.

Why doesn't the sign turn sideways on my tablet?

On some tablets and foldables with Android 16 or newer, the system decides the screen orientation and ignores the app. Turn the device by hand and the sign adjusts.

Features and quick access

How do I quickly show a message to start a conversation?

Long-press the TypeTalk icon and choose Introduction. Your first message is on the big display straight away. You can drag the shortcut onto your home screen and change the text in Settings → My first message.

How do I add TypeTalk to Quick Settings?

Swipe down from the top of the screen to open Quick Settings, then tap the pencil or Edit. Drag the TypeTalk tile in among the others. Tapping it opens TypeTalk with a new message.

How do I hide buttons I don't use?

In Settings → Buttons on the main screen, turn off the buttons you don't need and change the order of the rest with the arrows. Settings can't be hidden, so you can always bring everything back.

Why doesn't read aloud work, or why does it sound odd?

TypeTalk uses the text-to-speech voice installed on your phone. In your Android settings, search for "text-to-speech" and download a voice for your language.

Can TypeTalk turn what the other person says into text?

Right now a speech recognition app can do that in split screen with TypeTalk. The steps are on this page, in the Conversation both ways section.

In version 1.9 TypeTalk gets this itself, optional and offline. It's a free extra and isn't as accurate as paid services.

Privacy and your data

Do I need internet or an account?

No. TypeTalk works offline, without signing in, and asks for no permissions.

What does TypeTalk save, and does it send anything?

Your phrases, categories and settings are saved only on your phone. Optionally also your last message, which you can turn off in Settings. TypeTalk sends nothing over the internet and has no analytics.

More in the privacy policy.

What is Google backup, and do I need it?

If you turn it on, Android saves your phrases and settings to your Google Account once a day, so they come back on a new phone by themselves. It's off by default. Turn it on in Settings → Moving to a new phone. More in the Google backup section.

How do I move my phrases to a new phone?

On your old phone

  1. Open Settings → Moving to a new phone and tap Send all phrases to yourself.
  2. A list of apps opens. Choose your e-mail app and send the message to your own address.

On your new phone

  1. Install TypeTalk.
  2. Open that message. Long-press any word in it, then tap Select all.
  3. In the menu above the text, tap TypeTalk. If you don't see it, tap ⋮ or Share and choose TypeTalk from the list of apps.
  4. TypeTalk opens with your phrases. Tap Add to phrases, then Add.

The phrases go back into their categories. Any you already have are skipped, so nothing is doubled.

Do I lose my phrases when I update the app?

No. Updating keeps all your phrases, categories and settings.

How do I delete all my data?

Uninstall TypeTalk, or in your Android settings open Apps → TypeTalk → Storage and tap Clear data. The names differ slightly between phone makers. If Google backup was on, Android removes the copy in your Google Account by itself over time.

Privacy

TypeTalk doesn't collect your personal data, doesn't send it to us and doesn't share it with anyone. No account, no analytics, no ads. Your phrases and settings stay on your phone, unless you turn on Google backup yourself.

Google backup

Android can save apps' data to your Google Account, so it comes back by itself when you move to a new phone. This is an Android service, not part of TypeTalk: TypeTalk has no internet access and sends nothing itself.

Since version 1.6, backup is off by default for TypeTalk. The app asks you once whether you want to turn it on, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Moving to a new phone.

On: your phrases, categories and settings come back by themselves on a new phone when you sign in with the same Google Account. Google stores and keeps the backup, and we have no access to it.

Off: everything stays only on this phone. If the phone breaks or is lost, the phrases can't be recovered. So before you change phones, tap “Send all phrases to yourself” in Settings and choose e-mail or a notes app. A direct transfer from one phone to another, by cable or Wi-Fi when you set up the new phone, works even with backup off, because it doesn't go through Google.
